The big picture

Ecotime does one job: it turns raw clockings into payable hours. Everything else in the software is a setting that controls one step of that journey. Learn the journey first and the screens start to make sense.

Select a stage to see what happens there and which screens you use.

For support staff

Support desk

Most Ecotime calls are about one thing: the hours on screen don't match what the person thinks they worked. The cause is nearly always one of three places. Either the clockings are wrong, the wrong shift was applied that day, or an error is stopping the calculation. Pick what the caller is reporting to see the likely causes, most common first.

From docs stated in the official Eco Time documentation Support tip general time and attendance practice. Confirm it on the customer's setup.

Always check in this order

This is the same journey from The big picture, used as a checklist. Don't skip ahead to the shift rules until the clockings are right. Bad clockings make every rule look broken.

  1. Clockings for the day

    Open the employee's clockings for the date range. Are they all there, and do they alternate IN, OUT, IN, OUT? A hand icon means someone already edited that clocking by hand, and the original values are still shown.

  2. The shift Ecotime used

    Check the Daily Roster for that date. Is there a roster entry? If not, which payroll shift is the employee on? Did someone change it recently?

  3. Errors and warnings

    A red stop sign means hours aren't calculating properly yet. Clear the errors first. Warnings, such as a late start, don't block anything. They are only flags.

  4. Daily hours

    In Browse Daily Hours, look at which categories the time landed in. Is the overtime sitting in holding, waiting for authorisation?

  5. Payroll hours

    In Browse Employee Payroll Hours, check the period totals. Is leave there? Is the period starting on the right day?

  6. Process, then check again

    After any fix, press Process on the clocking, or use Reprocess Hours for a date range, and confirm the numbers changed.

Log the call

Ask the caller

  • Which employee number, and which exact date?
  • What did they actually work, start to finish, and did they take a break?
  • What does Ecotime show, and what did they expect?
  • Is it one person, a group, or everyone?
  • Did anything change recently, such as a new shift, a roster import or someone editing clockings?
  • Is the stop sign red or green?
  • When is payroll due? This sets how urgent the call is.

Breaks & clockings

Break problems come in two kinds. First, find out whether the site makes staff clock out and back in for breaks or deducts breaks automatically. Many sites do one or the other, and the fix is completely different.

Staff clock for breaks

Four clockings a day

IN 08:00→OUT 12:30→IN 13:00→OUT 17:00
  • Forgetting the break clock-in leaves OUT, OUT, which gives a same-direction error. From docs
  • An odd number of clockings means one is missing, or one is a double tap. Support tip
  • Fix it in the clockings list. Highlight the wrong one and press Ctrl+A or F2 to change its direction, or delete the duplicate. From docs
Breaks deducted automatically

Two clockings, rule takes the break

IN 08:00→rule −0:30→OUT 17:00
  • The deduction lives in the setup. That means the daily shift's rules or a macro script. The docs include an example called "Using AddClocking to deduct breaks". From docs
  • A CountTime macro can total the time out between clockings, for sites that cap or check break length. From docs
  • If the break is wrong for everyone on a shift, it's the shift or the macro. If it's wrong for one person, look at their clockings or their shift for that day. Support tip
  • Don't edit macro scripts without the customer's consultant. A mistake there affects every employee. Support tip

Ecotime today

The documentation pages are several years old. Eco Time's own website describes what the software does now. This chapter covers the software only. Your clocking devices come from your reseller and connect through Edit Clocks or an integration such as Impro, so the vendor's own hardware is left out.

What the time and attendance module does

Shifts

Shift handling

  • Automatic shift selection: picks the shift that best matches someone's clockings.
  • Rolling shifts and rostering or scheduling.
  • Handles night shifts, allowances and other complex calculations.
Control

Overtime & exceptions

  • Overtime authorisation (see Overtime).
  • A real-time attendance dashboard.
  • Exception-based management and exception reports.
Output

Reports & payroll

  • Automated reporting and a wide range of customisable reports.
  • Payroll integration, plus an API for BI tools such as Power BI.
  • An SMS function for alerts.

Automatic shift selection is a common cause of "wrong shift" calls. If the site uses it, check whether the clockings fitted a different shift better before you look at the roster.

Who can do what on the web interface

The web interface works in a browser, for both cloud and on-premise installs. What a person can do depends on their role.

Employee

  • Clock in and out from the browser.
  • Add daily hours. These stay pending until a supervisor approves them.
  • Apply for leave and check leave balances.
  • View their own clockings and daily roster.
  • Upload documents such as sick notes.

Supervisor

  • Approve or decline leave and daily hours for their group.
  • See clockings, hours (including authorised overtime), leave and exceptions on one screen.
  • Edit clockings from the employee window.
  • Roster their group and print reports.
  • View only for personal details.

Web administrator

  • Everything a supervisor can do.
  • Change employees' personal details.
  • Add new employees.

Other modules

App

Eco cell phone app

  • Android 8 and above.
  • Works offline and syncs when it reconnects.
  • Logs GPS for each clocking, with a Google Maps link. Geofencing isn't mentioned anywhere.
  • Leave and sick-leave requests.
  • An administrator must enable mobile access for each employee in the User Module.
Leave

Leave management

  • Electronic leave applications from the web or the app.
  • Automatic emails tell managers about applications and employees about approvals.
  • Tracks balances and leave accrual.
Alerts

SMS notifications

  • A licensed feature. Enable it in the license manager first.
  • Turn it on in Config›Bells and Features›SMS, then click Get Authorization and send the code to Eco Time to activate it.
  • Rules live in User Module›SMS›Notification rules.
  • Absent and late alerts fire at the trigger time.
  • Messages are limited to 160 characters and can go to the employee or their next of kin.
Payroll

Integrations

  • Standard modules for Sage 300, PaySpace, PeopleHR, Gallagher, Impro and HikCentral.
  • Any payroll with an import facility can take an export file.
  • An API for BI tools such as Power BI.
Costing

Labour / job costing

  • Labour cost by department or job.
  • Needs the Job Costing module.
  • Rules are under Setup›Job Costing›Costings.
  • Clocks can be set as job costing clocks, which adds a Job Costing tab.
People

HR, access & visitors

  • HR: training, disciplinary, equipment issued, dependants and employment history.
  • Access control: anti-passback, time-of-day rules, making staff clock before they leave, and movement reports.
  • Visitor management: a paperless visitor log.

Setting it up, in order

All setup lives in the Configuration Module. The docs stress that setting it up correctly avoids most later problems. The order matters because later items point at earlier ones. A payroll shift is built from daily shifts, and a daily shift is built from time categories.

  1. Switch on the features you need

    Under System Features you decide which classifications are active and turn on optional features such as overtime authorisation. Do this first so the related fields show up later.

  2. Classifications

    These are the labels you file people under: Company, Branch, Department, Cost Centre, Category, Payscale, Type and Workgroup. You only need the ones that match how your business is organised.

  3. Group Selections

    Groups are saved selections of employees, built from classifications. You use them to filter screens, run reports and do bulk tasks. You can also define groups by the days of the week people are available to work.

  4. Leave types and public holidays

    Leave types have to exist here before anyone can book leave against them. Public holidays let the processor generate paid holiday hours.

  5. Shift Time Categories

    These are the buckets hours are sorted into, such as Normal Time and Overtime. Ecotime ships with some that you can edit. If you plan to authorise overtime, you create the holding and authorised categories here (see Overtime).

  6. Shift Time Rates

    The rates attached to those categories. Setup›Shifts›Shift Time Rates

  7. Daily shifts

    You create one daily shift for each type of day people can work, such as an early, a late, a night or a day off. Each one has its expected times and its rules for sorting hours into categories. Setup›Shifts›Daily shifts

  8. Payroll shifts

    A payroll shift strings daily shifts together into a full pay period, such as a week of Mon–Fri days plus two days off. Set the Payroll Shift Start Day so the period starts on the right weekday. Every employee must be on a payroll shift, or Ecotime can't calculate their hours.

  9. Clocks (terminals)

    Register the clocking hardware with Edit Clocks. This tells Ecotime where clockings come from.

  10. Employees

    Add each person and give them their card, their classifications and, most importantly, their payroll shift.

Shifts & rosters

This is the part people get stuck on. There are two layers.

The pattern is the payroll shift. It is the employee's normal, repeating week, and Ecotime falls back on it every day unless told otherwise.

The roster is a one-off instruction for a specific date: "On Thursday the 14th, this person works the Late shift." A roster entry overrides the pattern for that day only. Use rostering when people work varying shift times with no fixed pattern, or for one-off changes such as a swap or an extra day.

Ways to roster someone

  1. One person, one day: the Daily Roster window

    Open Daily Roster to see and manage rostering for all employees. Switch to the Calendar option for a visual view over a chosen number of days from a start date. To change a day, right-click the day and pick a daily shift from the drop-down list. That adds a new daily roster entry for that date.

  2. Many people at once: Bulk Tasks

    The bulk menus let you add, swap or remove daily shift rosters for a whole group in one go. You can also add daily overtime authorisations for a group the same way.

  3. Supervisors: the web interface

    Supervisors can put an employee on a new rostered shift or change an existing one from Eco Time Web, without the desktop software.

  4. From a spreadsheet: Importing Daily Shift Rosters

    Rosters can be imported using the generic import format. Re-importing the same roster for the same period overrides what's already there, so you can correct a file and import it again.

Overtime: roster it, or authorise it

There are two ways to control overtime. They answer different questions. Rostering asks "did we plan this?" and authorisation asks "do we approve this?" You can use either one, or both.

Before the work · planned

Roster the overtime

If you know in advance that someone is coming in extra, put them on a rostered shift for that day, such as a Saturday overtime shift. Ecotime then expects them and their hours fall into that shift's categories.

  • Good for planned weekend work or cover.
  • Done in the Daily Roster window, by bulk roster, on the web or by import.
  • The manager decides ahead of time, not afterwards.
After the work · approval

Authorise the overtime

Extra hours go into a holding category and stay there, unpaid, until a manager approves them. Approval moves them into the authorised category that payroll picks up.

  • Good for stopping unapproved stay-backs from being paid automatically.
  • Can work per day (Daily Overtime Authorisations) or per pay period (Payroll Authorisations).
  • Hours that aren't approved don't get paid.

Normal time0.00

This is a simplified model for learning. Real daily shifts can also apply breaks, rounding and other rules, so your actual figures depend on how your shifts are set up.

Setting up overtime authorisation

  1. Turn the feature on

    In the Configuration Module's features, go to the Standard tab and tick Daily Overtime Authorisations, Payroll Authorisations or both.

  2. Create the holding category

    Add a Shift Time Category to hold worked-but-unapproved overtime. Leave its Requires Authorisation boxes unticked. That sounds backwards, but this is the category that holds hours waiting for approval. It is not the one that needs approving.

  3. Create the authorised category

    Add the category approved hours move into, and tick Requires Authorisation for the type you use (Daily and/or Payroll).

  4. Point your shifts at the holding category

    In your daily shift rules, send overtime to the holding category instead of straight to a paid overtime category.

  5. Approve

    In Browse Daily Hours (daily) or the payroll hours view (per period), select the record and press Ctrl+A. Everything in the holding categories moves to the authorised ones. For groups, use the bulk option to add daily overtime authorisations.

Remember: if authorisation is switched on, nobody gets paid for that overtime until someone approves it. Add approval to your routine before every payroll export.

    Fixing errors

    Ecotime shows a red stop sign while errors remain and a green one once they're cleared. All errors must be fixed before the payroll calculation runs for export. Nearly every error is one of these four.

    ErrorWhat it meansHow to fix it
    Missing clock-outThey clocked in but never out, so the shift is incomplete. Hours are only worked out once an OUT is found.Highlight the error, click Fix and add the missing OUT clocking by hand.
    Missing daily hoursNo hours for the day. This is usually a side-effect of a missing clock-out.Fix the missing clock-out first. This error normally clears with it.
    Two clockings, same directionFor example, IN then IN. Someone double-tapped, or clocked the wrong way.Highlight the error, then change the direction or delete the wrong clocking in the clockings window.
    Invalid shift codeThe employee is pointing at a shift code that doesn't exist or isn't valid.Check the employee's payroll shift and roster entries, and correct the code.

    No clockings showing at all?

    If the Global clockings window is empty, the usual cause is that the Server Module isn't running. On the Windows server, look for the service named EcoTimeServer, CSTimeServer or TNAServer and start it. If it's running and still nothing arrives, the clock may be offline. Contact your Ecotime supplier.

    A clocking outside the shift's times, such as a late start, gives a warning rather than an automatic change. Warnings feed the late-start, early-departure and absenteeism reports.

    Leave

    An employee with no clockings for a day counts as absent. If they were on holiday or off sick, record leave so the day is explained and paid correctly.

    1. Make sure the leave type exists

      Leave types are set up in the Configuration Module. You can't book leave against a type that doesn't exist.

    2. Add the leave

      On the Leave Option screen, click Insert to open Update Leave. Choose the leave type and set the From and To dates. Two entries of the same type can't start on the same date.

    3. Hours by hand, if needed

      The Add Daily Hours and Add Payroll Hours tabs let you type leave hours in directly. Insert and Change only become available once you pick a leave type on the General tab that allows manual hours.

    4. Employees can request it

      The Eco Mobile App has a Leave Request option where staff choose the leave type and dates themselves.
